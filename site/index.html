01the problem

A 64k intro cannot carry 3.4 MB of samples.

General MIDI is the cheapest way to write music: 128 instruments and a drum kit, known to every sequencer. Windows has the bank built in, but macOS, Linux and the browser do not, and the file is © Microsoft / Roland, so it cannot be shipped. The answer here: describe every wave with a small, fixed set of synthesis models, and let a no_std generator rebuild the bank at load time.

A GM wave is an attack and a short loop. The long decay is not in the wave: the note's volume envelope lives in the DLS articulation. Piano 1, waves 289–298, are 4.8 k–13.7 k samples: 0.2–0.6 s of attack, then a loop of 155–2272 samples. So a model only has to reproduce the first half second plus one steady loop, not a ten-second piano decay.

A loop of L samples is periodic in L, so its spectrum sits on the grid rate/L. The 1339-sample loop of wave 293 has a 16.5 Hz grid. Stretched piano partials and beating unisons are already quantised to that grid in the original. Every periodic family therefore renders its loop with integer phasors on exactly that grid. The loop is periodic by construction, with no click and nothing lost.

The structure is copied; only the waves are synthesised. Instruments, regions, key ranges, articulation, rates, lengths and loop points come from the original as a skeleton without audio. The output is a normal RIFF 'DLS ' buffer: any DLS player, the unisynth sampler, WaveSabre's Adultery and the browser DAW all play it unchanged.

02the model

Ten families. Four layers. 512 numbers.

A wave is up to four layers, each one of ten fixed model families, placed in a region: the exactly periodic sustain, a free-running attack that fades into the loop, or the whole one-shot. Parameters have ranges and defaults, and the build rejects anything over budget, so a parameter file can never smuggle in a waveform.

layers Σ→gain→harmonic correction→time × band tf→fine envelope env→soft knee 0.9→int16

Harmonic correction: dB gains at harmonics of f0, exact on the loop (DFT, gain per bin, inverse DFT) and a linear-phase FIR of at least four periods on the attack. tf: 20 log-spaced bands × up to 9 rows from 0 to 640 ms, applied by STFT to the attack only. env: up to 24 points from 0 to 400 ms, finer than the STFT can see.

Deterministic by construction. The generator uses only basic IEEE operations: its own sin, exp2, log2, an xorshift RNG seeded with the wave index, and a radix-2 + Bluestein FFT whose twiddles come from the same math. clippy bans f32 transcendentals and mul_add in the crate. The bank is byte-identical natively and in WebAssembly, built whole or in part.

03the fit

Measure first. Then search. Keep only improvements.

gmfit never searches blind. It reads the instrument's published physics as priors, analyses the original into a model it can already defend, lets each family propose candidates, and only then hands a narrowed box to CMA-ES. Every step must beat the committed result or nothing is written.

  1. Priors from the literature

    Every GM program and drum key maps to a prior: model kind, families to try, and physical starting points. Piano inharmonicity B, mallet mode ratios, Hammond footages, TR-808 hi-hat square frequencies, cowbell 540/800 Hz, the vowel formants of a choir. Search ranges are narrowed around them.

  2. Analysis before search

    Pitch on the loop grid, harmonic tables by cluster energy, unison and detune from split partials, inharmonicity, envelopes on windows of at least two periods. Loop vibrato and AM become per-harmonic tables, and the noise floor between partials becomes a periodic noise layer.

  3. Character analysis

    Not every sound is a tone. Onset detection and Morlet-wavelet band tracking find strikes, trills and glides (modal). Collision statistics and per-band ring times find shakers. Analytic-signal chirp tracking finds FM. LPC and syllables find voices, and the T60 band decay finds plucked strings.

  4. Candidates, then CMA-ES

    Each family proposes models; all are scored and the best by rank survives. Then a seeded, full-covariance CMA-ES (Hansen's formulation, rank-one + rank-μ, cumulative step size) tunes the explicit scalars inside the unit box. It is deterministic for a seed and runs in parallel.

  5. Analytic corrections

    What the models cannot express is corrected in closed form within budget: a harmonic gain curve (exact on the loop), a time × band gain grid on the attack, and a fine envelope that catches onset detail finer than the STFT. refine alternates fit and correction.

  6. Keep only improvements

    A result is written only if its rank, the score minus penalties for audible defects, beats the committed one, and report --check fails if any wave drops. Bulk fits run per GM category, and each round ends with listening.

04the judge

A score for the search. An ear for the score.

The optimiser needs one number. But one number averages away exactly what a listener hears: a buzz once per period, a missing noise floor, a 2 dB hole before the loop. So the score carries defect flags, and above it sits a hearing model built after PEAQ. Each listening round turned what the ear caught into a new measurement.

# similarity of the played signals
# (loop unrolled to 0.5 s, A-weighted)
score = exp(−( d_spec/W₁ + d_onset/W₂
              + d_env/W₃  + d_loop/W₄
              + d_per + d_mod ))
rank  = score − 0.1 · character flags
              − hearing excess

$ gmfit render 376      # standard kick
score 0.721  tier B   flags: lines

Tiers: A ≥ 0.85, B ≥ 0.70; any defect flag drops a wave to C.

  • d_specMean |dB| of log-mel spectrograms at three STFT sizes, A-weighted per band and floored 50 dB under the peak so inaudible differences do not count.
  • d_onsetThe same comparison on the first 50 ms only, so a kick's click weighs what the ear gives it instead of being diluted over half a second.
  • d_envRMS envelopes every 2 ms over the attack. On the loop, a held note's shimmer is a texture: smoothed shape plus wobble depth.
  • d_loopExact DFT magnitudes of one loop period, compared bin by bin up to Nyquist.
  • d_per · d_modPeriodicity (tone versus noise), and the strongest partial's flutter and pitch movement.
  • flagspitch_off (5 cents), env_off, loop_click, buzz (per-period peakiness), clipped, tone_off, mod_off, lines, transient_off.
  • hearOuter/middle-ear weighting, half-Bark bands, spreading, forward masking, Zwicker specific loudness. It reports what is added or missing beyond a JND, plus flutter, swell, roughness and ring time. It is calibrated so that two independent noises of the same texture count as identical.
05where it stands

Honest numbers.

Every wave of both sets is fitted, with no placeholders left. Reeds, pipes, organs and brass score highest: steady harmonic tones are what the families express best. Drums, chromatic percussion, ensembles and sound effects score lowest, because their attacks and noisy textures carry detail the models can only approximate. The scores are strict: a wave can sound right in a song and still score 0.6. Two real banks differ about as much from each other as the procedural bank differs from its target.

06listen

Four banks, one position, one key.

Every song is rendered through four banks by the same renderer with one gain per row: Windows gm.dls, the gmplus Sound Canvas soundfont, and the procedural bank fitted to each, gen-gm and gen-plus. Keys 1–4 switch banks without losing your place.

Every Windows PC since 98 has made General MIDI sound like this: a GS sound set Roland licensed to Microsoft, in 3.4 MB. It is the target of gen-gm, read from a local copy and never shipped.

archive.org BESTMID1–7 · 1994

Song credits go to their composers and arrangers, named in the song selector. The renders are for comparison only and are removed on request. Tools: Rust, Bun, Apple AUMIDISynth (renderer), LAME, Google Cloud Storage. Part of unisynth, the 64k-intro synth this bank was made for.
